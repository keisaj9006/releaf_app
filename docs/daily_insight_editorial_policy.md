# Releaf Daily Insight — Editorial & Evidence Policy

## Purpose

Daily Insight is a lightweight educational feature. Its job is to make one useful, evidence-informed idea memorable each day without turning Releaf into a medical-news feed or presenting population research as a personal diagnosis or prescription.

The feature must remain visually secondary to the core regulation actions on Home.

## Source hierarchy

Prefer sources in this order:

1. **Official evidence-based public-health or clinical guidance** — WHO, NIH/NHLBI/NCCIH/ODS, CDC, NHS/NICE where appropriate.
2. **Systematic reviews and meta-analyses** in established peer-reviewed journals, preferably indexed in PubMed/PMC.
3. **Large prospective cohort studies / randomized controlled trials** when a high-quality review is not available or when the study itself is the fact.
4. **University or research-institute summaries** only when they clearly link to the underlying study. The app should link to the primary paper whenever practical.

Do not use lifestyle blogs, commercial supplement sites, influencer content, news aggregators, SEO health pages, or unsourced social-media claims as evidence.

## Claim rules

Every insight must distinguish between:

- **Guideline / recommendation** — e.g. adults should generally aim for 150–300 minutes of moderate physical activity per week.
- **Association** — e.g. an exposure was associated with a lower or higher risk in observational data.
- **Intervention evidence** — e.g. randomized trials tested whether doing something changed an outcome.
- **Mechanistic / biological plausibility** — useful context, but not proof that an intervention improves a clinical outcome.

Never silently convert association into causation.

### Wording examples

Prefer:

- “was associated with a 23% lower risk…”
- “researchers observed…”
- “guidelines recommend…”
- “evidence suggests…”
- “trials have not consistently shown…”

Avoid:

- “proves” unless the source genuinely supports that strength of claim
- “prevents depression” from observational evidence
- “boosts serotonin” as a general wellness claim without direct support
- “extends your life by X years” when a study reports mortality risk rather than individual lifespan
- “the best bedtime is 10 PM” as a universal statement
- supplement doses, treatment instructions, or medication advice in Daily Insight

## Numbers and risk

Percentages must preserve the denominator and study context.

For relative-risk findings, the detail/evidence note should make clear that the number is population-level and does not predict an individual outcome.

Do not translate a relative-risk reduction into “years of life gained” unless the original study directly estimated life expectancy and the estimate is appropriate for the target population.

## Mental-health treatment claims

Daily Insight must not imply that a lifestyle behaviour, supplement, meditation, gratitude practice, breathing exercise, or soundscape is a replacement for professional assessment or evidence-based treatment.

For topics with mixed evidence, the nuance is itself valuable. Example: low vitamin D status is associated with depression in observational studies, while supplementation has not reliably been shown to prevent or treat depression across clinical trials.

## Copyright and paraphrasing

Releaf should report **facts and findings**, not copy prose.

For every entry:

1. Read the source and identify the supported claim.
2. Rewrite the claim from the evidence in Releaf’s own plain-language voice.
3. Do not reproduce distinctive sentences, headings, graphics, tables, or explanatory passages from the source.
4. Store the original source title, publisher and URL.
5. Keep the evidence note explicit enough that the user can understand important limitations.

Facts themselves are not protected in the same way as original expression, but attribution and careful paraphrasing are still required as editorial policy.

## UI requirements

Home card:

- compact, one fact only
- maximum two headline lines
- small source/evidence line
- visible info icon
- no alarmist styling
- no diagnostic or prescriptive CTA

Expanded sheet:

- full paraphrased explanation
- evidence type
- limitation / interpretation note
- source publisher
- original publication/title
- “Open original source” action
- educational-information disclaimer

## Rotation

The launch catalog should rotate deterministically by local calendar date so the fact is stable throughout a user’s day.

Avoid repeating closely related subjects on consecutive days when the catalog grows large enough to support editorial scheduling.

Target after launch: at least **30 reviewed insights**, then grow toward 60–90 so repeated content is infrequent.

## Review cadence

- Re-check URLs and claims before production release.
- Re-review guideline-derived facts at least every 12 months.
- Re-review faster when a source publishes a major guideline update or correction.
- Keep a source if it remains scientifically sound even when it is not new; recency is less important than evidence quality for stable health knowledge.

## Current preferred publishers

- World Health Organization (WHO)
- National Institutes of Health (NIH), including NHLBI, NCCIH and Office of Dietary Supplements
- Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)
- NHS / NICE for UK-facing guidance where relevant
- PubMed/PMC-indexed peer-reviewed systematic reviews and meta-analyses

## Product boundary

Daily Insight is not a live-news feed. Releaf should favour durable, useful scientific knowledge over novelty. A scientifically boring fact that reliably helps users understand sleep, movement, connection, stress, attention or wellbeing is more valuable than a sensational new study with weak evidence.
