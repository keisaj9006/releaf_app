# Reset Breathing Evidence Standard

Last reviewed: 2026-09-08

## Purpose

Keep Releaf breathing content useful, specific and evidence-aware without presenting
one breathing ratio as a universal treatment or "magic" nervous-system formula.

This document governs naming, educational copy and evidence claims for Reset
breathing sessions.

## Notation

Releaf always names phases explicitly.

- Continuous pattern: `inhale–exhale`, for example `4–6`.
- Pattern with holds: `inhale–hold–exhale–hold`, for example `4–4–4–4`.
- Do not publish ambiguous shorthand such as `4:1:2` without defining which
  phase each number represents.
- Equivalent ratios are not automatically equivalent pacing. For example,
  `2–4` and `3–6` are both 1:2 inhale-to-exhale ratios, but the full cycles
  are 6 seconds and 9 seconds respectively.

## Evidence hierarchy

### Broader support: voluntary slow-paced breathing

Systematic-review and HRV literature supports slow voluntary breathing as a
general method that can alter heart-rate variability and autonomic measures.
Preset protocols commonly use approximately 6 breaths per minute, while
individual resonance frequency can differ.

Relevant sources:

- Laborde S, et al. *Effects of voluntary slow breathing on heart rate and
  heart rate variability: A systematic review and a meta-analysis.*
  PMID: 35623448.
- Lehrer-style HRV biofeedback protocol review:
  *Methods for Heart Rate Variability Biofeedback (HRVB): A Systematic Review
  and Guidelines.* PMID: 36917418.
- *Comparing the Effects of Square, 4-7-8, and 6 Breaths-per-Minute Breathing
  Conditions on Heart Rate Variability, CO2 Levels, and Mood.*
  PMID: 39864026.

Product implication:

- It is reasonable to describe 5–5 and 4–6 as slow-paced breathing options.
- Do not imply that exactly 5–5 or exactly 4–6 is optimal for every user.
- Comfort and adherence matter; users should shorten the count when a pace
  feels effortful.

### Exact inhale/exhale ratio

Research does not establish one universally superior inhale-to-exhale ratio.

Product implication:

- Describe extended-exhale patterns as pacing choices.
- Do not claim that a 1:2 ratio uniquely "activates the vagus nerve",
  "switches on the parasympathetic nervous system", or treats anxiety.
- A method such as 3–6 may be described as placing more time on the exhale,
  with the caveat that the ideal ratio is not settled.

### Box breathing

Box/square breathing is a structured breathing method with breath holds.
There is emerging method-specific research, but the evidence base is narrower
than the broader slow-breathing literature.

Relevant sources:

- *Comparing the Effects of Square, 4-7-8, and 6 Breaths-per-Minute Breathing
  Conditions...* PMID: 39864026.
- *Box breathing and prolonged exhalation reduces markers of physiological
  stress reactivity...* PMID: 42388906.

Product implication:

- Present box breathing as a structured attentional/pacing option.
- Do not rank it as superior to continuous slow breathing.
- Always disclose the holds before the user starts.

### 4–7–8 breathing

4–7–8 has some method-specific clinical studies, but those studies do not
justify describing the exact numbers as a universal sleep or anxiety formula.

Relevant sources:

- *Effects of sleep deprivation and 4-7-8 breathing control on heart rate
  variability, blood pressure, blood glucose, and endothelial function in
  healthy young adults.* PMID: 35822447.
- *The Effect of Deep Breathing Exercise and 4-7-8 Breathing Techniques
  Applied to Patients After Bariatric Surgery on Anxiety and Quality of Life.*
  PMID: 36480101.
- 2026 tinnitus RCT: PMID 41676854.
- Comparative physiology study: PMID 39864026.

Product implication:

- Releaf may offer 4–7–8 as an optional bedtime pacing method.
- Copy must explicitly say the exact numbers are not a magic formula.
- Provide a no-hold alternative such as 4–6.

## Safety copy

Every breathing method with a deliberate pace should preserve these rules:

- The breath should stay comfortable rather than unusually deep.
- Stop the count and return to normal breathing if the user becomes
  light-headed, short of breath or more anxious.
- Breath holds are optional. They are not a progression milestone.
- Do not encourage forceful emptying of the lungs.
- During panic or air hunger, prefer a no-hold option instead of telling the
  user to push through a hold.

## Product-writing rules

Allowed:

- "slow-paced breathing"
- "extended-exhale pattern"
- "structured pattern with holds"
- "6 breaths per minute"
- "same 1:2 ratio, different overall pace"
- "may help some people settle"
- "research supports slow breathing overall"

Avoid:

- "activates your vagus nerve"
- "resets your nervous system"
- "switches off fight-or-flight"
- "clinically proven to cure anxiety"
- "the scientifically optimal breathing ratio"
- "432/528 Hz-style numeric claims applied to breathing"
- claims that one exact count works for everyone

## Current Releaf breathing map

- 5–5 Balanced — continuous, no hold, 6 breaths/minute.
- 4–6 Calm Rhythm — continuous, no hold, 6 breaths/minute.
- 3–6 Long Exhale — continuous, no hold, 1:2 ratio, ~6.7 breaths/minute.
- 4–4 Focus — continuous, no hold, 7.5 breaths/minute.
- 5–7 Slow Cycle — continuous, no hold, 5 breaths/minute.
- 3–3 Active Steady — continuous, no hold, 10 breaths/minute.
- 4–4–4–4 Box — structured holds, 3.75 cycles/minute.
- 4–7–8 — hold + extended exhale, ~3.16 cycles/minute.
- 4–5 Wired Steady — longer continuous practice, ~6.7 breaths/minute.

These values describe pacing only. They are not effectiveness rankings.
